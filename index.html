A global view with a Malaysian perspective

A world of emissions.

Where greenhouse gases come from depends on how we look at them. Country totals show global scale. Per-person figures and sector patterns reveal differences that a national ranking alone cannot show.

What are we measuring? CO₂-equivalent puts carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide and fluorinated gases on a comparable warming scale. The emissions shown here exclude land use and forestry. 2025 is the latest EDGAR data year.

Reading the global picture

Totals show the scale of emissions

Country totals include power generation, industry, transport and agriculture. Per-person figures help compare countries with different population sizes.

International aviation and shipping count towards the global total but are reported separately from countries.

02

A different picture per person

Per-person figures average national emissions across the population. Smaller countries can have substantial emissions per person even when their contribution to the global total is small.

Development groups

Why compare these eight countries?

Four developed and four developing countries were chosen to compare major emitters and different regions, including Malaysia and neighbouring Indonesia. These examples do not represent group averages.

Per-person emissions fell in all four developed countries but rose in the four developing countries. Malaysia increased from 4.9 to 9.7 tonnes per person.

Developed and developing follow the IMF's advanced and emerging/developing groups. The same classification is used for both years.

03

Global emissions grew as the leading contributors changed

Global emissions in 2025 were 68.5% higher than in 1990, despite temporary falls during major disruptions. The leading contributors also changed over time.

04

The sectors behind the country totals

Power and heat generation account for 29.2% of global emissions in 2025, the largest sector share. Each country's sector mix differs.

The comparison matters: power accounts for about 37% of Malaysia's emissions in 2025, and transport about 20%. Sector shares show where each country's emissions come from.

05

Malaysia’s place in the picture

Malaysia contributes 0.62% of global emissions but exceeds the global per-person average. Income and sector changes provide context for its emissions.

For Malaysia: emissions grew by 285.1% from 1990 to 2025. Power and heat generation added 109.6 Mt CO₂-equivalent, the largest sector increase.

Data definitions and coverage

Comparable greenhouse gases. Emissions include fossil CO₂, methane, nitrous oxide and fluorinated gases, converted using 100-year IPCC AR5 global warming potentials. Land use, land-use change and forestry (LULUCF) are excluded.

Units. Gt means billion tonnes. Mt means million tonnes. Per-person values are annual tonnes of CO₂-equivalent averaged across the population.

Global and country totals. International aviation and shipping are included in global totals and are reported separately from national totals. EU27 and the global aggregate are excluded from country rankings.

Country reporting areas. EDGAR merges some countries and territories. Map shapes follow those reporting areas where boundaries are available. Country labels and tooltips retain the combined reporting name. Tiny economies may not be visible at this map scale.

Missing data. Unmatched boundaries are grey. Source blanks are preserved. The eight economies selected for the sector heatmap have complete sector data for 2025.

Development and income. IMF development groups and World Bank income groups are different classifications. October 2026 IMF membership is held fixed for the historical comparison. World Bank income labels are used as retrieved in October 2026.

Joined World Bank data. Figure 9 includes 170 individual economies with positive GDP and population values, matched to EDGAR in 2025. EDGAR’s merged geographies are excluded from this join so that country boundaries remain comparable.

Population series. Per-person emissions follow EDGAR’s population normalisation. Bubble areas use World Bank population values. These population series can differ, so the bubble-area population should not be used to recalculate the displayed EDGAR per-person value.

Sector changes. Malaysia’s waterfall uses the difference between each sector’s 2025 and 1990 emissions. The sum of those changes equals the change in the national total.

Data retrieval. Prepared on 14 October 2026. World Bank indicator responses were last updated on 8 October 2026. Narrative figures refer to 2025 unless another year is stated.
