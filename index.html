Who emits most, what changed, and where Malaysia fits

A world of emissions.

Global emissions have kept rising, while countries have followed different
paths. Compare the largest contributors, changes since 1990 and sector
patterns in selected developed and developing countries, with a focus
on Malaysia.

What are we measuring? CO₂-equivalent compares greenhouse gases by their
warming effect. EDGAR figures exclude land use and forestry and
run through 2025. The export comparison uses OECD data for
2022.

Who contributes most to global emissions?

Beyond country totals

Population changes the comparison

A smaller country can emit less overall while having higher
emissions per person. National totals alone do not show this
difference.

The next section compares emissions across populations of different sizes.

02

Per-person figures reveal a different pattern

A large population can contribute to a large national total.
Per-person figures reveal another pattern, including different changes among the
selected developed and developing countries.

Development groups

Malaysia's per-person emissions nearly doubled

Malaysia rose from 4.9 to 9.7 tonnes per person, alongside
increases in China, India and Indonesia.

These examples are not group averages. The IMF's advanced and
emerging/developing classification is held fixed for both years.

03

Global emissions kept rising as the leaders changed

These different per-person paths occurred as global emissions grew 68.5%
between 1990 and 2025. The balance between leading contributors also
changed.

04

Power leads globally, but country sector mixes differ

Country rankings identify the largest contributors. Sector shares reveal the
activities behind those totals, with differences within both selected development
groups.

Malaysia's sector mix: power and transport together account for 57.5%
of its emissions. Their role becomes clearer when we trace
Malaysia's growth since 1990.

05

Power and transport lead Malaysia's emissions growth

Malaysia contributes 0.62% of global emissions. Its national total is
nearly four times its 1990 level, led by growth in
power and transport.

For Malaysia: power and transport lead its emissions growth. Its
small global share sits alongside above-average emissions per person and
production chains linked to buyers abroad.

Data definitions and coverage

Comparable greenhouse gases. EDGAR emissions include fossil CO₂, methane, nitrous
oxide and fluorinated gases, converted using 100-year IPCC AR5 global
warming potentials. Land use, land-use change and forestry (LULUCF) are
excluded.

Units. Gt means billion tonnes. Mt means million tonnes. Per-person
values are annual tonnes of CO₂-equivalent averaged across the population.

Global and country totals. International aviation and shipping are included
in global totals and are reported separately from national totals.
EU27 and the global aggregate are excluded from country rankings.

Country reporting areas. EDGAR merges some countries and territories. Map
shapes follow those reporting areas where boundaries are available. Country
labels and tooltips retain the combined reporting name. Tiny economies
may not be visible at this map scale.

Missing data. Unmatched boundaries are grey. Source blanks are preserved.
The eight economies selected for the sector heatmap have complete
sector data for 2025.

Development and income. IMF development groups and World Bank income
groups are different classifications. October 2026 IMF membership is held
fixed for the historical comparison. World Bank income labels are
used as retrieved in October 2026.

Joined World Bank data. Figure 5 includes 170 individual economies
with positive GDP and population values, matched to EDGAR in
2025. EDGAR’s merged geographies are excluded from this join so
that country boundaries remain comparable.

Population series. Per-person emissions follow EDGAR’s population normalisation. Bubble areas
use World Bank population values. These population series can differ,
so the bubble-area population should not be used to recalculate
the displayed EDGAR per-person value.

Sector changes. Malaysia’s waterfall uses the difference between each sector’s
2025 and 1990 emissions. The sum of those changes equals
the change in the national total.

Sector labels. Power includes heat generation; Industry covers fuel burned
in industry; Processes covers industrial production; Fuel means fuel production;
Farming means agriculture, excluding land-use change.

Emissions linked to exports. Figure 12 counts emissions across the
production chain for Malaysia's exported goods and services, including emissions
in Malaysia and abroad. It includes exports used in further
production and those sold for final use. Destinations are direct
buyers and may export the products again. These totals use
different boundaries from EDGAR's national totals and should not be
added to them. Retrieved on 22 October 2026.
